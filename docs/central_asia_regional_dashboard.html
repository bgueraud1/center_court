Our commitment

Why Center Court highlights new tennis regions.

§1

Tennis has expanded dramatically over the last decades, yet its competitive map is still shaped by a narrow set of long-established hubs—Europe, North America, Australia, and a few exceptions elsewhere.

§2

Because tennis stories are built around emerging talent, national pathways, and the sense that a player can carry a whole ecosystem with them, Center Court believes that regions sitting on the edge of the global Tour deserve far more visibility. Their players often travel farther, face thinner calendars, and compete with fewer resources, but they still deserve to be seen and understood.

§3

Central Asia is a perfect example of that challenge. Its tennis history has been written mostly by Kazakhstan, with a large share of its modern impact coming from players who were born in Russia or developed their game elsewhere. Even so, the region has produced real world-class moments on both the ATP and WTA tours. The structural obstacles are still severe—limited investment, fewer domestic opportunities, and fragile pathways for young players. As a symbolic sign of how thin the base remains, the Tour is still waiting for a lasting breakthrough from Tajikistan. Recent momentum, including player transfers and development efforts in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan, gives the region a plausible route toward a brighter future, and that is exactly the kind of story Center Court wants to follow.
